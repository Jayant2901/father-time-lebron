The idea, in three steps

How I turned "he doesn't age" into an actual receipt

Raw stats can't be compared across eras — a 1975 stat line and a 2024 stat line are basically different sports. So the pipeline never touches raw stats twice: it converts, then compares, then hands you the proof.

Step 1
Rank him against literally everyone that year

Every player-season becomes a 0–100 percentile among that year's qualifying players. 100 = best in the league that year; 50 = "you exist." Same trick baseball uses for OPS+.

Step 2
Figure out what "normal" even looks like

Every qualifying player-season at, say, age 27, gets pooled together into a mean and standard deviation — history's own definition of "a normal 27-year-old," no vibes involved.

Step 3
Catch him being unnatural

z = (his percentile − age's mean) ÷ age's std. ±1 SD raises an eyebrow. ±2 is basically a medical anomaly. LeBron casually lives at +1.5 to +1.8, every age, forever.

How much to trust this

Some ages barely have any receipts, ngl

The age baseline only pools players with several qualifying seasons, and only ages with at least 15 historical player-seasons get full confidence. Teenage and late-30s seasons are real but rare — I'm not going to pretend I'm sure when I'm not.

Sample counts are the real pooled cohort sizes behind each age's baseline (Player Efficiency Rating). Faded bars = below the 15-season confidence threshold.

The fine print

Things worth knowing (the nerdy stuff, but I tried)

  • I compare percentiles, not raw stats — a 1975 stat line and a 2024 stat line are basically speaking different languages.
  • A season only counts if the guy actually played (≥800 minutes, ≥40 games), and "normal for this age" only counts careers with 3+ qualifying seasons — no letting one hot cameo skew what's typical.
  • PER, Win Shares, BPM and VORP go back to 1949-50; True Shooting %, Usage % and friends only exist from 1996-97 on, because that's just when the data starts.
  • Cross-source player matching is name-based — a tiny sliver (~0.2% of rows) are real same-name mix-ups, logged and disclosed instead of swept under the rug.
